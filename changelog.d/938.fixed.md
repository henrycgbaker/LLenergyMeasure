A study that aborts abnormally no longer leaves an exited engine-server container behind. The safety net that runs when a study's process exits stopped the study's containers but did not remove them. That is enough for the experiment and baseline containers, which are launched with `--rm` and so are reaped by Docker the moment they stop; the engine-server container is deliberately launched WITHOUT `--rm`, because a server that crashes during startup must survive for `docker logs` to recover the diagnostic. An abnormal exit therefore left it on the host indefinitely, holding its writable layer and holding logs that nothing would read again. The net now stops the container, writes its log tail into the study's `failed-runs/` directory as `abandoned-container-<id>.log`, and then removes it. Removal is conditional: if the log tail cannot be read or cannot be written, the container is LEFT IN PLACE and a warning names it, because a stray container is removable by hand whereas the last record of why a study died is not recoverable. Cleaning up after an abnormal exit therefore takes longer per container than it used to, since reading and writing the log tail happen between the stop and the removal. The startup reaper that clears containers whose launching process is gone still stops them without removing: its filter is host-wide, so what it finds belongs to somebody else's abandoned study, whose evidence is not ours to destroy.
