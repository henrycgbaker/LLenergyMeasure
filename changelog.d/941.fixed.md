A server-mode run whose composite warmup gate did not converge quickly could spin indefinitely instead of timing out: the server stayed up and healthy, the GPU fell to idle, no request was served, and the `timeout_seconds` failsafe never fired. The gate's power-plateau observable runs a steady-state detector whose cost grows super-quadratically with the sample count, and its worst case is a series that is stable for a long stretch and then stops being stable at the very end. The gate re-ran that detector every two seconds over a series that grows for as long as warmup lasts, synchronously on the same event loop that issues the warmup traffic, so past a few minutes of warmup one evaluation cost more than the interval between evaluations. That stalled the traffic, which let the GPU fall to idle, which turned the series tail into the detector's worst case and made the next evaluation slower still; and the failsafe could not end it, because its deadline was checked by the loop the evaluation was blocking.

Two changes fix it. **The gate's evaluation now runs off the traffic-issuing loop**, which polls it for a verdict rather than blocking on one, so the warmup traffic keeps flowing and the failsafe deadline is reachable on every iteration however long an evaluation takes. **The power-plateau observable is evaluated on a 1 Hz view of the power series** instead of the sampler's full 100 ms cadence, which re-bases the detector's cost on the failsafe timeout in seconds rather than on the capture cadence. This is a behaviour change to the warmup gate's evaluation cadence, and it is disclosed in each result's `pre_window_protocol` provenance string, which now names the cadence alongside the thresholds.

What the downsampling does and does not change is worth stating precisely, because it applies to ONE of the three observables:

- The plateau's own thresholds are duration-based and survive it unchanged: the plateau window is a fraction of the series, so it covers the same wall-clock stretch at either cadence, and sub-sampling a stationary series leaves its coefficient of variation unchanged in expectation. Two sample-count constants in the same pipeline do change their time footprint, and both are accepted: the dropout median filter smooths over 3 s of the view rather than 0.3 s of the raw series (it exists to kill single-sample transients, and a 1 Hz view has none left to kill), and the detector's 8-sample history floor becomes 8 s rather than 0.8 s (never binding, because the gate already requires about 90 s of history before it can pass). The view is also blind to power ripple faster than its own Nyquist frequency, where the raw series reads such ripple as instability; the plateau question is whether power has settled at a level, not whether consecutive samples differ.
- The other two observables are NOT downsampled, deliberately. Both are extreme-value statistics over a trailing window - any active throttle bit, and the temperature range - and a decimated series is systematically blind to a short-lived extreme, so a brief throttle episode or temperature spike that should veto the window could pass unseen. They read the series at the sampler's full cadence, which costs about 2 ms per evaluation at the 9000 samples a 900 s warmup collects, against roughly 3.9 s for the plateau on its view.
- No calibrated threshold value and no gate decision rule changes.

Per-evaluation cost is bounded by `timeout_seconds`, not made constant: it is the same super-quadratic curve re-based on the timeout in seconds, about 3.9 s per evaluation at the 900 s default. `timeout_seconds` has no upper bound, so a much larger value scales that cost up sharply (roughly 30 s per evaluation at 1800 s). The gate stays responsive regardless, because the evaluation is off the loop and the deadline is checked every poll, but a discarded evaluation's worker thread does keep occupying a core until it finishes, which can overlap the start of a measured window.

The energy measurement is unaffected: the sampler keeps its full cadence, the downsampling exists only inside the plateau observable, and warmup energy is still integrated over the whole series. One consequence worth knowing about: a warmup that would previously have hung now converges or times out, so gate convergence times can appear for runs that used to produce nothing at all.
