A server-mode run whose composite warmup gate did not converge quickly could spin indefinitely instead of timing out: the server stayed up and healthy, the GPU fell to idle, no request was served, and the `timeout_seconds` failsafe never fired. The gate's power-plateau observable runs a steady-state detector whose cost grows super-quadratically with the sample count, and its worst case is a series that is stable for a long stretch and then stops being stable at the very end. The gate re-ran that detector every two seconds over a series that grows for as long as warmup lasts, synchronously on the same event loop that issues the warmup traffic, so past a few minutes of warmup one evaluation cost more than the interval between evaluations. That stalled the traffic, which let the GPU fall to idle, which turned the series tail into the detector's worst case and made the next evaluation slower still; and the failsafe could not end it, because its deadline was checked by the loop the evaluation was blocking.

Two changes fix it, and the second is why the first is enough. **The gate's three observables are now evaluated on a 1 Hz view of the power series** instead of the sampler's full 100 ms cadence, which bounds the sample count by the failsafe timeout in seconds rather than by the capture cadence. This is a behaviour change to the warmup gate's evaluation cadence, and it is disclosed in each result's `pre_window_protocol` provenance string, which now names the cadence alongside the thresholds. No calibrated threshold and no gate decision changes: every threshold the gate applies is defined over a duration rather than a sample count (the temperature and throttle windows are in seconds, the plateau window is a fraction of the series), so the decimated view asks the same question of the same span; sub-sampling a stationary series leaves its coefficient of variation unchanged in expectation; and 1 Hz still clears the detector's four-sample floor by two orders of magnitude over the 90 s of history the gate already requires. **The evaluation also runs off the traffic-issuing loop**, which is polled for a verdict rather than blocked on one, so the warmup traffic keeps flowing and the failsafe deadline is reachable on every iteration however long an evaluation takes. Traffic that never stops also means the series tail never falls to idle, so the expensive regime stops being reachable at all.

The energy measurement is unaffected: the sampler keeps its full cadence, the downsampling exists only inside the gate, and warmup energy is still integrated over the whole series. One consequence worth knowing about: a warmup that would previously have hung now converges or times out, so gate convergence times can appear for runs that used to produce nothing at all.
